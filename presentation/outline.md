## 1. The question
- Can a Bayesian sequential model forecast football match outcomes, and how far is it from the closing market?
- Scored with RPS, Brier and log-loss on a chronological holdout, never on training data.
- Forecasts are made at T-60, before kickoff, so no post-match information is used [2].

Caveat: the written research question is not stated in FINDINGS; this slide is framed from its data and baseline sections.

## 2. Data and pipeline
- 8 leagues x 7 seasons (2019-20 to 2025-26): 56 league-seasons, 19,145 valid matches [1].
- 80% / 20% chronological split: training 15,316 matches (to 2025-02-01), test 3,829 (2025-02-01 to 2026-05-24) [1].
- Transfermarkt lineups: 19,140 of 19,145 fixtures strictly matched; Gate A passed on test, 3,829 of 3,829 matched (100.00%) [1].

Caveat: Transfermarkt is a frozen upstream snapshot (current to 6 July 2026); the date our local files were downloaded is not recorded [7].

## 3. Baselines and the market gap
- Stage 1 static Dixon-Coles RPS 0.2057245; Stage 2 sequential filter RPS 0.2060203 [2].
- Closing market average RPS 0.1926041 on the same 3,545 rows; Stage 2 trails it by 0.0134162 [2].
- The dynamic filter did not beat the static model on RPS (Stage 2 is slightly worse) [2].

Caveat: the market uses later information than T-60, so it is a benchmark, not a fair opponent; Stage 1 skips 284 test matches with unseen teams [2].

Graph: `figures/baselines_rps.png` (RPS bars, Stage 1, Stage 2, market).

## 4. What failed and why
- Surprise mechanisms (Stages 3-5): validation chose CHANGE_SCALE 0 and sensitivity 0, so Stage 3 and Stage 5 equal Stage 2; Stage 4 (sign-inverted, superseded) log-loss difference -0.0010106, CI [-0.0025560, 0.0001155], includes zero [3].
- Lineup Gate B FAILED (2026-10-19): c_att +0.0950, CI [0.0487, 0.1413]; c_def -0.0022, CI [-0.0413, 0.0368] [4].
- Diagnosis: true null for the defence term, weak defence proxy as secondary; the one post-hoc attack-only run did not improve log-loss (+0.000109, CI [-0.000669, +0.000878]) [4].

Caveat: failures are reported as final; Gate B's verdict stands and the post-hoc attack-only run is exploratory. FINDINGS does not restate the gate's pass criterion [4].

Graph: `figures/gate_b.png` (c_att and c_def with CIs).

## 5. P1 on the one-shot TEST window
- P1 (learned process noise 0.005, initial variance 0.3, cold-start defence 0.3, rate clip, rho -0.04) was selected on DEV and VAL only [5].
- Pre-registered rule met: log-loss 1.008321 to 0.995493, difference -0.012828, 95% CI [-0.015519, -0.010091] over 3,829 rows [6].
- P1 closes 26.54% of the RPS gap to the closing market (Brier 26.85%, log-loss 27.47%) [6].

Caveat: the test window is not fresh. It was examined during Stages 1-5 before P1 was scored on it once, so this is a retrospective holdout, not a clean out-of-sample result [6, 7].

Graph: `figures/p1_test.png` (log-loss difference with week-block CI).

## 6. The descriptive split
- 1 of 3,829 rows has a baseline rate outside [0.15, 6.0] and contributes -0.02% of the total gain [6].
- The 3,828 normal rows carry the gain: log-loss 1.008578 to 0.995744 [6].
- On VAL (selection period) 11 rows carried 36% of the log-loss gain, so a concentrated gain was a known risk [5].

Caveat: this split was run after scoring and is descriptive, not a test; DEV and VAL numbers are selection-period, not out-of-sample [5, 6].

## 7. The forward test
- Started 2026-10-26 and pre-registered in `forward/PREREGISTRATION.md`, committed before the first forecast [8].
- Before each match the frozen Stage 2 and P1 forecasts are appended to an append-only, hash-chained log; fixtures are logged only when kickoff is more than three hours away [8].
- The first forecasts are logged; none is scored yet.

Caveat: FINDINGS records the start but not how many forecasts are logged, so no count is shown. It names a pre-registered minimum of scored matches without stating it; below that minimum the report is descriptive only, with no inference [8].

## 8. Limitations
- Market odds are a benchmark only, and snapshot-odds timing is known at schedule level only (weekend games Friday afternoon, midweek Tuesday afternoon) [7].
- The test window was examined before P1 was scored; one post-hoc lineup run exists; `home_adv` carries slight look-ahead inside the training window [7].
- The `--lineup` path is verified only by tests and the end-to-end check; a pytest run once re-ran an already-scored comparison and the output was restored, with no parameter, row or rule changed [7].

Caveat: the pytest incident is logged in DECISIONS.md; say it plainly rather than waiting to be asked [7].

## 9. What we claim and what we don't
- We claim: P1 improves log-loss over Stage 2 on the TEST window by -0.012828 (CI [-0.015519, -0.010091]), under a pre-registered rule, retrospectively [6].
- We claim: surprise scaling and lineup weighting gave no reliable gain (Gate B FAILED, stays failed) [3, 4].
- We don't claim: that P1 beats the market (it closes 26.54% of the RPS gap, not all of it), that the TEST result is out-of-sample, or any forward result [6, 7, 8].

Caveat: the claim is as strong as the pre-registration; a changed model would need a new model id and a separate log [8].

## 10. Takeaway
- One line: a pre-registered, retrospectively tested filter improvement narrows, but does not close, the gap to the closing market, and the forward test will say whether it holds.

Caveat: the forward test has no scored matches yet, so the takeaway is a hypothesis about the future, not a result [8].
